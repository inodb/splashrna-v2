What to enter

One target per FASTA record: a header line beginning with >, then the target itself. Several records can be submitted at once. 

> TP53
7157
> by accession
NM_001127233.1
> a sequence of my own
ATGTAAATAACTTTAATAAAACGTTTATAGCAGTTACACAG

Each record can be one of three things:

  • A human Entrez gene ID. Guides are designed against the intersection of that gene's RefSeq isoforms — the stretch common to all of them — so a guide knocks down every isoform. Human reference hg38; off-targets are checked against hg38 and mm10.
  • A RefSeq accession (NM_…). The current sequence is fetched from NCBI, and guides are designed against that single transcript.
  • A nucleotide sequence of A, C, G, T or N. Tiles containing N are skipped.

To turn gene symbols into Entrez IDs, the DAVID Gene ID Conversion tool works well: paste your list, choose your identifier type, pick Gene List, then convert to ENTREZ_GENE_ID.

Options

Predictions per gene
How many of the top-scoring guides to show. Default 5.
Remove guides with EcoRI or XhoI sites
Drops guides whose 97-mer contains GAATTC or CTCGAG, which complicate cloning. On by default; switch it off and such guides are kept but flagged.
Cut at the polyadenylation site gene IDs only
Ignores sequence downstream of the most proximal annotated cleavage site, which is not in the mature mRNA. On by default. Where no polyA site is annotated, the coding sequence is used instead, or the untrimmed transcript if there is no CDS either — the results say which.
Target the coding sequence only gene IDs only
Excludes the 5′ and 3′ UTRs.
Keep guides that repeat within the transcript
A guide matching the target gene in more than one place is dropped by default; tick this to keep it.
Keep guides that hit other genes
A guide whose 22-mer exactly matches another human or mouse gene is dropped by default; tick this to keep it, with the matching genes listed.
How thoroughly to check for off-targets
Checking the best few hundred candidates is much faster and almost always gives the same list. Every candidate checks all of them, which is what the original server did.

Reading the results

Position
Where the guide's target sits in the transcript, counted from its start.
Antisense guide
The 22-nt guide strand — what you order.
SplashRNA
The combined score. Guides scoring above 1.0 are generally strong; above 2.0, very strong. If the best score for a target is low, see the warnings below.
miR-30 and miR-E
The two underlying classifiers. The miR-30 score gates: the miR-E score only contributes when miR-30 exceeds 1.1.
Off-target
How many other human and mouse genes contain this exact 22-mer, with their Entrez IDs linked to NCBI. unique means none. A dash means the off-target index is not deployed here, so nothing was checked.
97-mer construct
The full miR-30 construct containing the guide, ready to order.

The transcript diagram

For a gene ID, the results include a diagram: one lane per RefSeq isoform with coding blocks in dark blue and UTRs in light blue, the designed region in green, and a tick at the genomic position of each predicted shRNA. Hover a tick for its sequence, position and score.

These are the 2015 annotations the predictions were computed against, not current RefSeq, so that the picture agrees with the numbers beside it.

Exon junctions

In an intersection sequence an asterisk (*) separates non-contiguous blocks. Guides are never designed across one, since that 22-mer does not exist in any real transcript.

Warnings

The best predicted score is below 1.0
Strong guides usually score above 1.0. A low best score often means the input was short, only a small part of the gene is shared across isoforms, or the sequence is very GC-rich. Supplying a longer sequence, or a single isoform by RefSeq accession, often helps.
The transcript is shorter than 1 kb
Little sequence to choose from. Picking the isoform most relevant to you usually gives better guides than the intersection.
EcoRI or XhoI site in the 97-mer
Shown when you have chosen to keep such guides. They can still be used, but cloning is harder.
Occurs more than once in this transcript
Shown when you have chosen to keep in-gene repeats.
Exact 22-mer match in other genes
Shown when you have chosen to keep off-target hits. Expect knockdown of the listed genes as well.
Could not find annotation for polyadenylation site
No polyA site is annotated for this gene, so the coding sequence was used instead — or the untrimmed transcript where there is no CDS.

The downloaded CSV

Download CSV gives everything in the table plus the full construct: Feature, shRNA.name, Position, Antisense.Guide.Sequence, SplashRNA, miR30.score, miRE.score, 97mer.construct, Warnings, and the off-target counts and Entrez IDs for each genome.

If you are comparing against output from the old server: it loaded the human and mouse tables into the wrong variables, so its Human and Mouse columns were swapped. Filtering was unaffected — a guide was dropped if either matched — but the species labels on archived CSVs are the wrong way round. These are correct.
